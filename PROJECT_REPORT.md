## 1. Introduction

Footprinting and reconnaissance are information-gathering activities used to understand an organization’s or system’s observable digital presence. Network scanning is a related assessment activity used, within an authorized scope, to identify reachable hosts and exposed network services. In a security assessment, these activities support asset awareness and help inform subsequent defensive review.

This internship exercise is titled “Footprinting, Reconnaissance, and Network Scanning.” Its repository is an evidence-oriented submission rather than a software application: it contains screenshots, a supporting document, and this report, but no source code or reproducible scan configuration.

## 4. Technical approach

A sound, authorized workflow for this class of exercise can be described in the following stages:

1. **Authorization and scope definition.** Confirm written permission, identify the approved assets and time window, and define prohibited actions before collecting information.
2. **Footprinting and reconnaissance.** Gather only information permitted by the assessment scope; distinguish publicly available observations from assumptions and record their sources.
3. **Network-scanning assessment.** Within the approved environment, use the authorized assessment method to examine reachable assets and exposed services. Record the tool and version, target scope, relevant settings, time, and observed output so another reviewer can understand the process.
4. **Evidence handling.** Preserve relevant screenshots and notes, label evidence clearly, and redact credentials, personal information, or other sensitive material before sharing.
5. **Analysis and reporting.** Separate observed facts from interpretation, describe limitations, and recommend defensive follow-up only where supported by the evidence.

The repository currently provides visual artifacts but does not document enough configuration or output details to reproduce a scan. The stages above therefore describe the technical context and reporting standard; they are not a record of specific commands or tools used in this exercise.

## 7. Ethical and safety considerations

All reconnaissance and scanning must remain within explicitly authorized scope. Do not scan third-party systems without permission, attempt to bypass access controls, or publish sensitive operational details. Store evidence securely, redact personal or confidential information, and use findings to support defensive learning and remediation.
